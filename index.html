01 / The lens

Whose story
becomes history?

The Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922) was a major phase of India's anti-colonial struggle. But national politics was not the only arena in which people challenged colonial power.

“To understand nationalism, we must also ask what freedom meant in the everyday lives of ordinary people.”

02 / Case files

Four stories.
Different struggles.

These movements did not all begin in 1921, nor were they all separate from Non-Cooperation. Explore their distinct origins, demands and relationships with wider nationalist politics.

Awadh · 1921–1922 · Peasant mobilisation

The Eka Movement

Peasant resistanceLocal leadership

Associated with Madari Pasi and other local leaders, the Eka movement grew amid agrarian tensions and demands involving rents, dues and landlord authority.

CASE 02
Chotanagpur · From 1914 · Adivasi movement

The Tana Bhagats

Adivasi historyChanging alliances

Associated with Jatra Oraon, the movement had religious, social and agrarian dimensions. By 1921, sections connected with Gandhi's struggle.

How did an Adivasi community define change and freedom?
CASE 03
Malabar · 1921 · Agrarian & anti-colonial rebellion

The Mappila Rebellion

Peasant grievancesContested history

Rooted in a complex setting of agrarian relations and colonial power, the rebellion unfolded in the context of Khilafat and Non-Cooperation. Its course also involved serious communal violence.

How do historians explain events with overlapping causes?
CASE 04
Awadh · 1920–1921 · Rural organisation

Awadh Peasant Struggles

Kisan SabhasAgrarian justice

Peasant organisation addressed high rents, illegal cesses, forced labour and insecure tenancy. Local leadership could be connected to, yet not fully controlled by, wider nationalist campaigns.

Did every Indian imagine freedom in the same way?
03 / Chronology

Not one moment.
A longer history.

1921 is the exhibition's focal point, but these movements have different timelines. The chronology makes overlaps visible without pretending they began together.

1914

Tana Bhagat movement begins
Associated with Jatra Oraon in the Chotanagpur region.

1920

Non-Cooperation gathers momentum
Peasant organisation and agrarian protest develop in several regions alongside wider nationalist politics.

1921

A year of overlapping struggles
The Mappila Rebellion takes place in Malabar; Tana Bhagat participation connects with Gandhian nationalism; peasant mobilisation continues in Awadh.

1921–22

Eka movement
Peasant mobilisation in parts of Awadh continues into 1922.

1922

Non-Cooperation is suspended
The national campaign changes course while local agrarian and social questions remain.

Dates are broad historical markers. Consult the linked scholarship for precise local sequences and differences.
